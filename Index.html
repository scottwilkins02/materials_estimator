How the plasterboard numbers are worked out

1. Wall area

Perimeter 2 × (length + width) × ceiling height, minus door/window area (quantity × the sizes set under "Advanced"), gives net wall area.

2. Board layout, not area ÷ board area

The wall run is unwrapped into one continuous strip and tiled with whole boards in both possible orientations — standing tall (fewer horizontal joints) and turned on their side (used when a wall is taller than one board). Whichever needs fewer whole boards is used, the same call a plasterer makes on site.

Openings only remove a whole board where their combined area could plausibly free one up (⌊opening area ÷ board area⌋) — conservative, since offcuts are often reused elsewhere.

3. Ceilings, wastage

An included ceiling is tiled the same way as a flat rectangle. Wastage allowance = net area × wastage %; gross area = net + wastage (a reference figure). The board count itself applies the same wastage % on top of the tiled total, rounded up.

Limits

  • Openings are deducted by area/count only — the diagrams are illustrative, not a cutting plan.
  • Each room is calculated independently; offcuts aren't shared between rooms (the safer assumption for ordering).
How the tile numbers are worked out

1. Which surfaces

Walls are tiled up to the height you set per room (defaults to full ceiling height, but editable for splashbacks/half-height tiling). The floor is optional and tiled separately as a flat rectangle.

2. Layout, both orientations tested

Same approach as boards: the wall run (or floor rectangle) is tiled with the chosen tile size in both orientations, and whichever needs fewer whole tiles is used.

3. Openings capped to the tiled height

Because walls can be tiled to a partial height, a door or window is only deducted up to min(opening height, tiled wall height) — a window entirely above a half-height splashback contributes nothing to the deduction. This is more precise than the plasterboard sheet, where boards always run full height.

4. Boxes

Boxes required = tiles required ÷ tiles per box, rounded up — set "tiles per box" to match what you're actually buying.

Limits

As with boards, opening deductions are area/count-based, not positional, and each room is calculated independently.

How the multi-finish plaster numbers are worked out

Why there's no layout optimisation on this sheet

Multi-finish (skim) plaster is trowelled on wet, not cut to size — there's no board or tile to orient, so unlike the other two sheets this is a straightforward gross area ÷ coverage per bag calculation, rounded up. Applying a tiling algorithm here would be pretending precision that doesn't exist for a wet trowel finish.

Coverage default and sourcing

The default (10 m² per 25 kg bag at 2 mm) is British Gypsum's published figure for Thistle Multi-Finish, confirmed across several independent retailer listings. One retailer FAQ page states "1.5 m² per bag" for the same product, which conflicts with every other source found (including one citing the manufacturer's own product data sheet) and reads as a typo rather than a genuine spec — moderate-to-high confidence the 10 m²/2 mm figure is correct, but check the bag or datasheet for your actual product, since coverage varies by brand and by how heavily you build up the coat.

Thickness scaling

If your application thickness differs from the reference thickness, coverage is scaled proportionally: effective coverage = reference coverage × (reference thickness ÷ your thickness). This is a linear approximation — real coverage vs. thickness isn't perfectly linear at the extremes, so treat it as a reasoned estimate, not a guarantee.

Openings and ceilings

Door/window area is deducted from wall area the same way as the plasterboard sheet (full opening height, since skim is a full-height finish). Ceilings are optional per room, since multi-finish is commonly skimmed onto both.
